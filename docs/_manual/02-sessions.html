The login screen is Aurora Greeter, a GTK 4 app that runs on greetd, the login manager. It shows the Aurora logo and wordmark, the time and date over the current wallpaper, and the users of the computer.

Logging in

  1. Click your name, or choose Other… and type a user name.
  2. Type your password and press Enter or Log In.

A wrong password shows Wrong password. Please try again. The login password also unlocks your keyring, where apps keep saved passwords (Wi-Fi, browsers, Git).

Choosing a session

When more than one session is installed, a menu at the bottom of the login screen lists them. Aurora installs two:

The choice is remembered. See Desktop sessions for the differences.

Restart and shut down

Restart and Power Off are in the corner of the login screen.

Automatic login

Chosen in the installer, or later in Settings → Users → Log in automatically. greetd then starts your session without asking for the password at boot. The keyring stays locked until an app needs it, and it asks for your password once.

Under the hood

  • greetd runs on virtual terminal 7 (/etc/greetd/config.toml).
  • Its default session is /usr/libexec/aurora-greeter-launch, run as the system user _greetd: it reads the system language and keyboard (/etc/default/locale, /etc/default/keyboard) and starts the greeter in a locked-down labwc. If the graphical greeter cannot start, greetd's text greeter agreety takes over.
  • After a successful login greetd runs the chosen session's command: aurora-session wayfire or aurora-session labwc (see What happens when you log in).

Aurora's desktop (top bar, dock, desktop, Spotlight, notifications) is the same in both sessions. What changes is the compositor, the program that draws windows on the screen and handles input.

Choosing the compositor

  • At the login screen, pick the session.
  • Or write wayfire or labwc in ~/.config/aurora/compositor: the Aurora session then uses that compositor.
  • Try Aurora OS (safe graphics) in the boot menu always uses labwc.

Automatic fallback

If Wayfire exits with an error within 15 seconds of starting, the session starts labwc instead, and you get a working desktop. The reason is saved in ~/.local/state/aurora/compositor-failure.log and shown in Settings → System Health. On graphics without 3D that expose a render node (VirtualBox or VMware without 3D, virtio-gpu without virgl), Aurora uses labwc from the start, because Mesa cannot draw there for Wayfire.

Configuration

Both compositors take their settings from Aurora's Settings app. labwc's configuration is ~/.config/labwc/rc.xml (keyboard shortcuts, input devices, window behavior); Wayfire's, ~/.config/aurora/wayfire.ini, is generated from the same preferences by aurora-wayfire-config each time they change. Edit rc.xml (or use Settings) rather than wayfire.ini.

A session is started by /usr/bin/aurora-session, Aurora's session manager. It is a short shell script; this is what it does, in order.

1. Environment

  1. Language. Reads /etc/default/locale, then your own choice from Settings in ~/.config/aurora/locale.conf. If the formats come from another language than the interface (for example English text with Italian formats), day and month names follow the interface language while numbers, units and currency keep the region's.
  2. Keyboard. Reads /etc/default/keyboard, then ~/.config/aurora/keyboard.conf, and exports the layout for the compositor (XKB_DEFAULT_LAYOUT and friends).
  3. Paths and toolkits. Sets the login PATH (including ~/.local/bin) and the variables that make apps use Wayland:

Apps started through D-Bus or systemd get the same values: the session hands them to dbus-update-activation-environment when it starts.

2. Graphics

/usr/libexec/aurora-render-env chooses renderers that work on this machine: the GPU when there is one, software rendering in virtual machines without 3D (and the extra settings Hyper-V's display needs).

3. The compositor

  1. Picks Wayfire or labwc (session choice, ~/.config/aurora/compositor, safe graphics, the graphics check above).
  2. On first login copies the default labwc configuration to ~/.config/labwc/, and writes the GTK stylesheet imports for the window style (aurora-look).
  3. For Wayfire, renders ~/.config/aurora/wayfire.ini and starts it, watching for an early failure (see fallback).

4. Startup programs

The compositor runs Aurora's startup script (/usr/share/aurora/labwc/autostart), which:

  1. passes the environment to D-Bus and systemd;
  2. creates the standard folders (Desktop, Documents, Downloads…) if missing;
  3. applies the display layout saved in Settings (~/.config/aurora/displays.sh);
  4. starts Aurora Shell under a watchdog: if the shell ever exits, it starts again, at most five times a minute, until you log out (its log is $XDG_RUNTIME_DIR/aurora-shell.log);
  5. starts the administrator password prompt (the polkit agent);
  6. runs aurora-autostart, which starts the apps in XDG autostart (yours in ~/.config/autostart and the system's in /etc/xdg/autostart), honoring OnlyShowIn, NotShowIn, Hidden and TryExec; manage them in Task Manager → Startup Apps or Settings → Apps;
  7. starts aurora-idle (screen off, lock and suspend on inactivity);
  8. opens the installer when you chose “Install Aurora OS”, or the Welcome app the first time you log in.

5. The shell

Aurora Shell draws every part of the desktop as Wayland layer surfaces: wallpaper and desktop icons, widgets, top bar, dock, notifications, on-screen indicators, Spotlight, overview and hot corners. It also runs the notification server, the system tray host, clipboard history, Night Light, the dynamic wallpaper, the weather, Storage Sense and screen time. Running aurora-shell again with a command talks to the running shell (see Shell commands).

Logging out

Logging out writes a marker so the watchdog doesn't restart the shell, ends the compositor, and greetd shows the login screen again.

Locking the screen

Press Super+L, use the lock button in the Aurora menu or the Control Center, or let it lock when idle. The lock screen (gtklock in Aurora's style) shows, straight on your current wallpaper, a big clock and the date, and under them your account picture (or your initial), your name and the password field: type the password and press Enter or the arrow. After 20 seconds without input only the clock stays; move the mouse or press a key to bring the rest back. A wrong password or Caps Lock shows as a small colored note under the field. Its background, and whether it shows your name and picture, are chosen in Settings → Appearance → Lock Screen. Your apps keep running.

On inactivity

aurora-idle watches for inactivity with swayidle and follows Settings → Power and Privacy & Security:

  • after the Screen blank delay (Settings → Power): the screen locks, if Automatic screen lock is on;
  • 15 seconds later: the screen turns off; moving the mouse or pressing a key turns it on;
  • after the Automatic Suspend delay (separately on battery and when plugged in): the computer suspends;
  • before suspending, the screen locks.

The live session never locks or suspends by itself.

Keep Awake

The Keep Awake tile in the Control Center stops screen blanking, locking and automatic suspend until you turn it off: for presentations, long downloads or reading.

Apps that hold off sleep

Video players and calls can ask the system not to sleep. When you choose Restart or Shut Down while an app is holding it off, Aurora says which app and offers Restart Anyway or Shut Down Anyway.

The power choices are at the bottom of the Aurora menu (the logo at the top left) as round buttons, and in the Control Center behind the power button:

Sleep
Suspends to memory. Open the lid or press a key to wake up; the screen is locked.
Restart
Closes every app and restarts the computer.
Shut Down
Closes every app and turns the computer off.
Log Out
Closes your apps and returns to the login screen.

Restart and Shut Down happen right away, after a short goodbye sound; save your work first. If an app is holding them off, Aurora tells you which and offers to go ahead anyway (see Apps that hold off sleep). Aurora plays a sound when you log in and when you shut down, restart or log out; turn it off in Settings → Sound → Startup and shutdown sounds.

The power button and the laptop lid do what Settings → Power says (suspend, shut down, nothing, or ask).
