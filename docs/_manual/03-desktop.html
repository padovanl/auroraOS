From top to bottom:

Top bar
The Aurora menu, the windows of the app in front, the tray, the Assistant (✦), search, status icons and the clock. See Top bar.
Desktop
Your wallpaper, the files in your Desktop folder and the widgets.
Dock
Your favorite and open apps, Launchpad and the Trash. See Dock.

Over these, when called:

  • Spotlight — tap Super: find and start anything.
  • Launchpad — every app as a grid.
  • Control Center — Super+S: volume, networks, quick toggles.
  • Calendar and notifications — click the clock.
  • Overview — Super+W: every window at once.
  • Snap Layouts — Super+Z: arrange windows side by side.
  • Aurora Assistant — Super+Shift+Space, when Aurora AI is on.

Everything follows your choices in Settings → Appearance (light or dark, accent color) and Settings → Desktop & Dock (layout, positions, sizes).

By default the bar runs edge to edge. Settings → Desktop & Dock → Top Bar → Style → Floating turns it into rounded islands of frosted glass held off the screen's edge: the menu and windows on the left, the clock in the middle (when it's centered), the status icons on the right.

Left side

Aurora menu (logo)
Who you are, system-wide actions and the power buttons. See Aurora menu.
Windows
One button per open window, minimized ones included (dimmer). Click to bring a window forward; click the focused one to minimize it. When an app has several windows, a number shows how many; click it for a list (numbered, to switch) and New Window, Minimize, Close and Quit.
Activities
Shows file transfers, Dev Hub installs and AI downloads while they run, with their progress.

Right side

System monitor (optional)
Processor and memory at a glance; its popover adds temperature, download and upload speed and free disk space. Turn it on in Settings → Desktop & Dock → Top Bar.
Tray
Icons of apps that run in the background (Discord, Slack, Steam, Nextcloud, KDE Connect…). Click for their menu.
✦ Assistant
Opens the Aurora Assistant (when Aurora AI is on).
Search
Opens Spotlight.
Status icons
Network, volume, Bluetooth, battery (with percentage if chosen), a red dot while recording the screen, and “…”. Click any of them for the Control Center. Scroll over them to change the volume (with the on-screen indicator); middle-click to mute.
Show Desktop
The thin line at the far right end of the bar, as on Windows: one click hides every window, a second click brings back the ones it hid. Turn it off in Settings → Desktop & Dock → Top Bar.
Clock
Date and time (24- or 12-hour in Settings → Date & Time; seconds and the clock's position in Settings → Desktop & Dock → Top Bar). Click it for the calendar, weather and notifications.

Menus opened with the keyboard close with Esc. The bar can be at the top or the bottom of the screen (Settings → Desktop & Dock).

Click the Aurora logo at the left of the top bar.

Your name and computer
Your picture (from Settings → Users), name, the computer's name and the Aurora version. The lock button next to it locks the screen (Super+L).
About This Computer
Settings → About: device name, processor, memory, graphics, disk and versions.
System Settings
Settings (Super+I).
App Center
Install and update apps.
Dev Hub
Developer tools in one click.
System Health
Settings → System Health: problems and their fixes.
Task Manager
What's running, end tasks, performance (Ctrl+Shift+Esc). See Task Manager.
Sleep, Restart, Shut Down, Log Out
See Shutting down.

Open it with Super+S or by clicking the status icons in the top bar. Close it with Esc or by clicking outside.

Header

The time and date, and the battery charge on laptops (⚡ while charging).

Sliders

Volume
Drag the knob (the sliders share one card at the top); the level shows in percent. Click the speaker icon on its left to mute. The arrow at its end lists the output devices (speakers, headphones, HDMI, Bluetooth) and, below them, the volume of each app playing sound (see Sound and Volume Mixer).
Microphone
When a microphone is present; the arrow chooses the input device.
Brightness
On laptops and screens that allow it.

Toggles

Grouped in three cards: Connections (Wi-Fi, Wired, Bluetooth, Airplane Mode, Mobile Hotspot), Modes (Do Not Disturb, Keep Awake, Power Mode) and Screen (Dark Style, Night Light, Screen Keyboard, Screen Recording). Each shows a round icon, filled with the accent color while on, its name and its state. Click the row to switch it; a small arrow opens a list under the cards.

Wi-Fi
On or off; the arrow lists networks (click one to connect, a password field appears for protected ones) and links to Network Settings.
Wired
Shown when a cable is connected; opens Network Settings.
Bluetooth
On or off; the arrow lists paired devices (click to connect or disconnect), with the battery charge of connected headphones, mice and keyboards that report it. The tile shows the first connected device and its charge.
Power Mode
Performance, Balanced or Power Saver.
Night Light
Warmer colors (schedule in Settings → Appearance).
Dark Style
Switches light and dark; choosing by hand stops the automatic sunset/sunrise switch.
Do Not Disturb
Silences notification popups (critical ones still show); they stay in the list.
Keep Awake
No screen blanking, locking or automatic suspend until you turn it off.
Airplane Mode
Turns Wi-Fi and Bluetooth off together.
Mobile Hotspot
Shares this computer's internet connection over Wi-Fi, like a phone's hotspot (on computers with Wi-Fi). The arrow shows the network's name (the computer's name followed by “Aurora”) and its password, made up the first time and kept (click it to copy), and, while on, a QR code: point a phone's camera at it to join. Change the name or password with gsettings set org.aurora.desktop hotspot-name / hotspot-password.
Screen Keyboard
Shows or hides the on-screen keyboard (for touch screens and tablets). It types into the app you were using without taking its focus: ⇧ for a capital (twice for Caps), ?123 and =\< for numbers and symbols, Ctrl for shortcuts (Ctrl, then C), arrows, and ✕ to hide it. Its look is chosen in Settings → Accessibility → Keyboard theme (ten themes). Also aurora-shell osk show|hide|toggle.
Screen Recording
Starts recording the screen (after the panel closes) or stops it; the arrow chooses the whole screen or an area, and whether the computer's sound is recorded. Recordings go to ~/Videos/Screencasts.

Media

When something is playing (a browser, Spotify, Celluloid…), its title, artist and previous / play-pause / next.

Bottom row

Battery time, then Screenshot (of an area), Settings, Lock and the power menu (Suspend, Restart, Power Off, Log Out).

Click the clock in the top bar.

Calendar

Today's full date and a month calendar (arrows change month and year). Open Calendar opens GNOME Calendar with your events.

Weather

The temperature, the sky and the next hours, for:

  1. the city chosen in the Weather app, if any;
  2. otherwise Weather's automatic location (found through GeoClue, rounded to about 10 km);
  3. otherwise the main city of your time zone.

Choosing a city in Weather updates it at once. Units follow Weather's setting, else your region. Data comes from Open-Meteo (no account, only that approximate position is sent) and is kept for 30 minutes. Click the weather to open the Weather app. Turn it off in Settings → Privacy & Security.

Notifications

New notifications pop up at the top right for a few seconds; critical ones stay. Click one to open the app that sent it; buttons on it run its actions. The popover keeps the history: Clear empties it, Do Not Disturb silences the popups.

When a notification contains a one-time code (“Your verification code is 482913”), it gets a Copy 482913 button.

Per-app choices (mute an app's notifications) are in Settings → Notifications.

Warnings Aurora sends by itself

  • Updates ready, and a restart needed to finish them;
  • the battery low (10%) and critically low (5%), and Battery Saver turning on;
  • a Bluetooth device low on battery: headphones, a mouse or a keyboard at 15% or less (once, until it has been charged again);
  • a disk nearly full, a drive plugged in.

Automatic Do Not Disturb

Like Windows' Focus Assist, Do Not Disturb can turn on by itself (Settings → Notifications → Automatic Do Not Disturb):

  • On a schedule, from and to an hour you choose (22:00–07:00 by default; off until you turn it on);
  • While an app is fullscreen: games, videos and presentations (on by default);
  • While sharing or recording the screen (on by default).

Critical notifications always show; the others wait in the list.

What's in it

  • Launchpad (the grid icon): every app.
  • Pinned apps, then open apps that aren't pinned. One mark under an icon for each open window (up to three): a dot, and a short bar in your accent color for the window you're in.
  • Minimized windows get their own icon near the Trash; click to restore.
  • Trash: click to open; right-click for Empty Trash. Drop files on it to delete them.

Using it

Click
Starts the app, or brings its window forward (a click on the window in front minimizes it); with several windows, each click brings the next one forward.
Middle-click
Starts another window of the app.
Rest the pointer
On an app with several windows, shows them; click one to pick it.
Right-click
The app's windows, New Window, the app's own actions (for example a new private window), and Keep in Dock / Remove from Dock.
Drag
Reorders pinned apps. To pin an app that isn't open, right-click it in Spotlight → Keep in Dock.

Style

Settings → Desktop & Dock → Dock → Style:

Floating
The default: one rounded shelf of frosted glass, held off the screen's edge.
Islands
The same shelf split in three pills of glass — Launchpad, your apps, the Trash — with a gap between them, as the top bar does in its floating style. The gap replaces the hairline before the Trash; the one between pinned and open apps stays. A pill with nothing in it (the Trash turned off) isn't drawn.
Full-width panel
A taskbar edge to edge, squared off against the screen's border, with no magnification.

Magnification, automatic hiding and the gap to the screen's edge work the same in Floating and Islands.

Settings

Settings → Desktop & Dock: position (bottom, left or right), the style above, icon size, magnification when pointing, automatic hiding, and which apps are pinned. With magnification off, the icon under the pointer lights up instead. Desktop Profiles can change these together (Work, Gaming, Battery).

Right-click a result

  • Files: open in Files, copy the path, or hand to the Assistant.
  • Projects: open a configured workspace (editor, terminal and Files together).

Choose what Spotlight searches in Settings → Desktop & Dock → Search.

Click the grid icon in the dock, or pinch with four fingers on a touchpad. The dock stays usable over it. Click outside or press Esc to close.

Sections

Apps are grouped, not one long alphabet. Frequently Used comes first: the six apps you used most over the last seven days, most used first, from what Screen Time counted (turn Screen Time off in Settings → Privacy and the section disappears). Then one section per kind of app, each in alphabetical order:

Internet
Browsers, mail, chat and anything else that goes online.
Office
Documents, spreadsheets, presentations, the calendar and contacts.
Photos, Music & Video
Pictures, players, the camera, editors.
Games
Everything from Game Hub and any game you install.
Development
Editors, development environments, Dev Hub's tools.
Utilities
Everything else, and the system's own tools: Settings, Files, Task Manager, Disks…

An app goes to the first section its own categories match (the Categories line of its launcher), so an app is never listed twice; a tool that happens to use the network (a package manager, a monitor) stays under Utilities instead of Internet. A section with no apps in it isn't shown.

Start typing to search instead: the sections give way to the same results Spotlight shows.

Apps you install from App Center, Dev Hub, Game Hub, Flatpak or apt appear here as soon as their launcher is installed.

Overview

Press Super+W, swipe up with three fingers, or use a hot corner (bottom left by default). Every open window becomes a card over a blurred picture of the screen.

  • Type to filter by app or title; Enter picks the first.
  • Click a card to switch to it; arrows move between cards.
  • × or a middle-click closes a window.
  • Show Desktop minimizes every window (also: swipe down with three fingers, or a hot corner, bottom right by default).

Workspaces

Workspaces are separate desktops for separate tasks. There are four by default; change the number in Settings → Multitasking.

  • Super+1…9: go to a workspace.
  • Super+Shift+1…9: move the window there.
  • Ctrl+Alt+←/→: previous / next.
  • Swipe sideways with three fingers.

Hot corners

Push the pointer into a corner of the screen to run an action. By default the bottom-left corner shows all windows and the bottom-right shows the desktop. Choose for each corner in Settings → Multitasking → Hot Corners:

The files and folders in your ~/Desktop folder appear on the wallpaper.

  • Select: click; draw a rectangle on empty space for several; Ctrl-click adds or removes one; click empty space to clear.
  • Open: double-click.
  • Move: drag an icon anywhere; positions are kept between sessions.
  • Drop: drag files from Files onto the desktop to copy them there.
  • New icons fill columns from the top left (or right, Settings → Desktop & Dock), folders first. App launchers (.desktop files) show their app's name and icon.
  • Up to 40 icons fit; with more, the last spot reads N more… and opens the Desktop folder in Files, where everything is.

Desktop menu

Right-click empty space: New File…, New Folder…, Open Terminal, Open Files, Edit Widgets…, Change Background…, Display Settings, Settings.

The desktop starts empty: widgets are there when you add them.

Adding and arranging

  1. Right-click the desktop (or a widget) → Edit Widgets…. Open windows step aside (they're minimized and come back when you finish).
  2. The gallery at the bottom has one tab per group. Click a widget to add it: it goes to the first free spot, from the right edge inward. Scroll the row with the mouse wheel.
  3. Drag widgets anywhere; a guide shows where they will land, and they line up with their neighbours.
  4. × on a widget's corner removes it.
  5. Done ends editing.

Right-click a widget for Customize… (its own options), Remove Widget and Widget Settings…. Clicking most widgets opens the related app.

Widgets follow the accent color, light or dark style, the clock format and units. Their positions are fractions of the screen and their sizes follow its height, so they keep their place and proportions when the resolution changes. Turn them all off in Settings → Desktop & Dock.

Every widget

To-do lists and notes are kept in ~/.local/share/aurora/widgets/; the layout in the desktop-widget-list setting.

Settings → Appearance.

Background

On top, a preview of your desktop. Personalize your background chooses what fills it:

Dynamic (follows the sun)
The Aurora landscape changes through the day (dawn, day, dusk, night) with the sun where you are, and crossfades between them. Series picks which of the three included sets of pictures it uses: Starfall (the default, with the Milky Way at night), Veil (misty valleys) or Horizon (calm, simple ridges). Each of the twelve pictures can also be chosen on its own as a Picture, or shown in a slideshow.
Picture
Recent images shows the pictures used lately; Browse Photos… picks any image (a copy goes to ~/.local/share/backgrounds); All Wallpapers… opens a searchable gallery of every wallpaper, which only draws the thumbnails in view, so it stays quick with thousands of pictures.
Picture of the day
A new background every day from Bing, NASA's Astronomy Picture of the Day or Wikimedia Commons' Picture of the Day, with its title and credit under the preview. Aurora asks the service for today's picture once a day and keeps the last week in ~/.local/share/aurora/daily; nothing else is sent.
Slideshow
Pictures from a folder of your choice (its subfolders too), or the included ones, changing every 15 minutes, 1 hour, 6 hours or 24 hours, in order or shuffled. Hidden folders are skipped; in a very large folder only the first 20,000 entries are looked at, and pictures added later join within 10 minutes.
Solid color
One of twelve colors, or any color with the color button.

Wallpapers are looked for in /usr/share/backgrounds, ~/.local/share/backgrounds and ~/Pictures/Wallpapers. The lock screen shows the picture on screen, unless you choose otherwise in Lock Screen, under Background: Same as the desktop, The desktop's, blurred (a soft, darker copy, made once per picture) or A picture of its own; and Show my name and picture, which can be turned off so the lock screen shows only the time, the date and the password field. The login screen, which comes before any account's settings, shows the Starfall series at the time of day.

Style

  • Color scheme: Light, Dark or Auto (dark from sunset to sunrise where you are). It changes the apps and the desktop widgets; the desktop itself — the top bar, the dock, Spotlight, Launchpad, the Control Center, notifications — stays dark glass over your wallpaper in both, as one piece.
  • Accent color: nine colors, used by buttons, selections, switches, widgets and the Control Center.
  • Aurora's own apps (Files, Settings, Task Manager, Dev Hub…) have surfaces with a faint violet tint, in both styles, and switch with the style at once; other apps keep their own colors.
  • Icons: Aurora (light and dark variants), Papirus, or any installed icon theme.
  • Pointer and its size.
  • Animations (all interface animations) and Window animations (opening, closing, minimizing windows; files floating in when a folder opens).

libadwaita, GTK 3, GTK 4 and Qt apps all follow the style and accent color.

Fonts

Interface, document and monospace fonts and sizes, scaling, anti-aliasing and hinting. Aurora uses Inter for the interface, JetBrains Mono for code, and Noto for every script.

Night Light

Warmer colors in the evening: from sunset to sunrise, on your own schedule, or all the time; choose the warmth. It changes the screen's color tables, which the graphics driver has to allow: virtual machines and a few drivers don't. Then Aurora says so with a notification and on the Night Light settings, instead of silently doing nothing; on a computer, installing the graphics driver (Settings → Updates → Additional Drivers) usually fixes it.
